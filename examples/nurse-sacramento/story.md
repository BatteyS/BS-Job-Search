# Why this field

I chose cardiac nursing after a summer volunteering at a free clinic, where I saw how much a calm triage nurse changes a frightening night for someone with nowhere else to go.

# What I'm looking for next

I want to move from the bedside into clinical informatics, so that the monitoring and charting work I have led on one unit can reach every unit in a hospital.

I would also take a clinical educator role, because training nurses is the part of every project I have enjoyed most.

# Career moves, in my words

- From telemetry to the cardiovascular ICU: I wanted sicker patients and more autonomy, and the ICU let me learn post-surgical care that the step-down unit only saw at the tail end.
- From staff nurse to charge and super-user work: the projects I volunteered for kept turning out to be about systems and teaching, and that is where I want my next role to sit.

# Stories worth telling

When our unit piloted continuous capnography, nobody owned the charting. I wrote the checklist, trained the night shift myself, and walked the unit at 3 a.m. to see where it broke down. Charting compliance went from 71% to 98% in four months, and the hospital is now rolling the checklist out to its second ICU.

During the 2023 Epic upgrade I was a super-user. I wrote tip sheets for the flowsheet changes and spent go-live week on three units, and I learned that most of the issues nurses logged were about where things had moved, not whether they worked. That week is why I started the informatics certificate.

# How I write

When I train a new nurse, I tell them the monitor is only as good as the person reading it. I would rather a nurse call me about a number that looks wrong than chart it and move on.
