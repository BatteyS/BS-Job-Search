# Why this field

I came to product marketing from an agency, where I spent three years writing messaging for products I never got to see used. I wanted to sit next to the people building the product and find out whether what I wrote was true.

# What I'm looking for next

I want to lead a product marketing team at a B2B software company that is past finding product-market fit and now has to explain itself to a bigger market. I want to own positioning and launches, and have a real voice in pricing.

# Career moves, in my words

- From the agency to Cobalt Freight Systems: I wanted to stay with one product long enough to see a launch play out over a year, not a quarter.
- From Cobalt to Brightline Analytics: Brightline had no product marketer, and building the function from nothing was the job I had been preparing for.
- From product marketing manager to senior at Brightline: the role grew as the company did, from writing collateral to running the win/loss program and co-leading pricing.

# Stories worth telling

When we repositioned Brightline's core platform, sales wanted a new tagline. I spent six weeks interviewing 38 customers and prospects who had chosen a competitor instead, and the answer was not the tagline: buyers did not want more dashboards, they wanted the software to make a decision for them. We rebuilt the story around decision automation, and our win rate against the main competitor rose from 24% to 37% over the next three quarters.

The 2024 pricing redesign started as a fight about discounts. The win/loss interviews showed that per-seat pricing punished our best customers for adding users, so I made the case, with the heads of product and finance, for usage tiers. Average contract value rose 18% the following year, and churn did not move.

# How I write

A launch brief should fit on one page. If it does not, the team has not decided what the launch is for yet, and no amount of collateral will fix that.

# Keep out of letters

- Salary history.
- The reorganization at Cobalt Freight Systems.
