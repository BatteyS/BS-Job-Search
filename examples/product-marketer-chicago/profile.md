# Career Profile — Avery Chen (fictional example)

This file is what /job-scan scores against. It is written for the scorer, not for an
employer, so it can be candid. /setup writes a first version from an interview; after
that it is the user's, edited by hand.

Nothing here goes into a resume or a letter. Resume facts live in `content/master.md`.

## 1. Identity and hard constraints

- **Senior product marketing manager, eleven years in.** Three years at a B2B marketing
  agency, then product marketing at two B2B software companies. Currently leads product
  marketing for a supply-chain analytics platform and manages two people.
- **Location:** Chicago, or fully remote within the US. Hybrid is fine if the office is in
  Chicago. Not relocating.
- **Pay:** at least $165,000 base.
- **Company:** B2B software with a real product organization — roughly Series B or later,
  or public. Avery does not want to be the first marketer again.
- **Travel:** up to about 25%.

## 2. Evidence bank

The strongest evidence, for the scorer's reference. `content/master.md` has all of it.

- **Positioning:** repositioned a platform from 38 customer and lost-deal interviews; win
  rate against the main competitor 24% → 37%.
- **Launches:** led the company's largest release of 2023, $3.1M pipeline in a quarter;
  built the launch process the company still runs.
- **Pricing:** co-led a move from per-seat to usage pricing; average contract value +18%,
  churn flat.
- **Programs:** built a win/loss program from nothing; battlecards for five competitors.
- **People:** hired and manages two product marketers; promoted one.

## 4. Scoring rubric

### 4.1 What makes a role worth applying to

Avery's own test: *"Will I own positioning and launches for something that matters to
the company, with a seat in pricing decisions?"*

**Scope beats title.** A "Senior PMM" role that owns the flagship product's positioning
and reports to the CMO is better than a "Director" title over a single feature area.

### 4.2 Score up

- Ownership of positioning, launches, and pricing input for a core product.
- Building or leading a team, or a clear path to it.
- B2B software sold to operations, supply-chain, logistics, or analytics buyers, where
  the domain knowledge transfers.
- Postings that mention win/loss, competitive intelligence, or pricing — the strongest
  evidence.

### 4.3 Score down

- Roles that are mostly sales enablement or content production with "product marketing"
  in the title.
- Developer-tools or deeply technical products where the posting wants an engineering
  background.
- Companies under about 100 people: the first-marketer job Avery has already done.

### 4.4 Location

Chicago carries no penalty. Remote takes a small one (−2): genuinely welcome, but a
Chicago office makes leading a team easier.

### 4.5 Hard passes

- Agencies and consultancies.
- Consumer and consumer-packaged-goods brand marketing.
- Demand generation, marketing operations, field or event marketing, and social media
  roles.
- VP and C-level roles — a stretch Avery is not making yet.
- Crypto, gambling, and tobacco.

keep the agency years to a line or two unless the posting is about research.
